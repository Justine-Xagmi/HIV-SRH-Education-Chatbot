\## 1. Purpose



This document provides clear, accessible educational information about HIV prevention. It explains how HIV can be transmitted, how transmission can be reduced, and where people can seek appropriate health services.



The information is educational. It does not diagnose HIV, replace an HIV test, prescribe medication or replace advice from a qualified healthcare professional.



\## 2. What Is HIV?



HIV stands for Human Immunodeficiency Virus. It affects the immune system, which helps the body fight infections and diseases.



HIV can be managed with effective antiretroviral treatment (ART). People living with HIV can live long and healthy lives when they receive appropriate treatment and care.



There is currently no generally available cure for HIV, but effective prevention, testing and treatment services can greatly reduce its impact.



\## 3. How Is HIV Transmitted?



HIV can be transmitted when certain body fluids containing HIV enter another person's body. These fluids include blood, semen, pre-seminal fluid, vaginal fluids, rectal fluids and breast milk.



Common routes of transmission include:



\- Sexual contact involving exposure to HIV without effective prevention.

\- Sharing needles, syringes or other injecting equipment contaminated with HIV.

\- Transmission from a mother living with HIV to her child during pregnancy, childbirth or breastfeeding, when effective prevention and treatment measures are not in place.

\- Exposure to infected blood through unsafe medical procedures or contaminated equipment.



HIV is not transmitted through ordinary everyday contact, including:



\- Hugging or shaking hands.

\- Sharing food, plates or drinking glasses.

\- Using the same toilet.

\- Sitting or working together.

\- Mosquito or other insect bites.

\- Sharing ordinary household items.



People should not be isolated, discriminated against or treated unfairly because of their HIV status.



\## 4. Ways to Prevent HIV



HIV prevention involves using appropriate prevention methods and accessing reliable health services.



\### 4.1 Condom Use



Correct and consistent use of condoms during sexual activity can significantly reduce the risk of HIV transmission.



Condoms can also help prevent several other sexually transmitted infections and unintended pregnancies.



Educational points:



\- Use a new condom for each sexual act.

\- Check the package and expiry date before use.

\- Follow the instructions for correct use.

\- Use compatible lubricants where appropriate.

\- Do not reuse condoms.



Condoms reduce risk but do not provide absolute protection in every circumstance.



\### 4.2 HIV Testing



Knowing one's HIV status is an important part of HIV prevention.



HIV testing can help people make informed decisions about prevention and access appropriate treatment and support.



Educational points:



\- People can access HIV testing through appropriate health facilities and approved testing services.

\- Testing should be voluntary and conducted with appropriate confidentiality and informed consent.

\- A recent exposure may require follow-up testing according to national testing guidance.

\- A negative result does not necessarily rule out a very recent infection because tests have different window periods.

\- A person with a reactive screening result needs the recommended confirmatory testing process.



The chatbot must not interpret individual test results or replace professional testing and counselling services.



\### 4.3 Pre-Exposure Prophylaxis (PrEP)



PrEP stands for pre-exposure prophylaxis.



It involves using antiretroviral medicines by people who do not have HIV to reduce their chance of acquiring HIV before possible exposure.



PrEP is an additional HIV prevention option for people who may benefit from it.



Important information:



\- A person needs an appropriate HIV assessment before starting PrEP.

\- PrEP must be accessed through an authorized healthcare service.

\- The medicine and schedule must follow current national guidance and the healthcare professional's instructions.

\- Follow-up appointments and HIV testing are important while using PrEP.

\- PrEP does not prevent pregnancy or most other sexually transmitted infections.



The chatbot must not recommend a specific PrEP medicine, dosage or schedule without an approved, current clinical source and appropriate clinical governance.



\### 4.4 Post-Exposure Prophylaxis (PEP)



PEP stands for post-exposure prophylaxis.



It involves taking antiretroviral medicines after a possible exposure to HIV to reduce the chance of infection.



PEP is time-sensitive.



\- Seek medical attention immediately after a possible HIV exposure.

\- PEP should be started as soon as possible, ideally within 24 hours and no later than 72 hours after exposure.

\- A healthcare professional must assess the exposure and determine whether PEP is appropriate.

\- When prescribed, the full course must be completed according to the healthcare professional's instructions.

\- Follow-up HIV testing is required according to applicable guidance.



\*\*Important safety message:\*\* Anyone who may have been exposed to HIV within the past 72 hours should seek urgent medical assessment. The person should not wait for symptoms or rely on the chatbot to decide whether PEP is needed.



\### 4.5 HIV Treatment and Undetectable = Untransmittable (U=U)



Antiretroviral treatment (ART) helps people living with HIV control the virus and protect their health.



People living with HIV who take ART and achieve and maintain an undetectable viral load do not transmit HIV to their sexual partners. This is commonly explained as Undetectable = Untransmittable (U=U).



Important educational points:



\- HIV treatment is important for the health of the person living with HIV.

\- Regular clinical monitoring helps assess treatment effectiveness.

\- Maintaining an undetectable viral load requires continued treatment and appropriate follow-up.

\- People should not stop or change their treatment without consulting their healthcare professional.



U=U specifically concerns sexual transmission when an undetectable viral load is achieved and maintained. It should not be generalized to other transmission routes without appropriate clinical guidance.



\### 4.6 Voluntary Medical Male Circumcision



Voluntary medical male circumcision can provide partial protection against heterosexual acquisition of HIV among men.



It does not provide complete protection and does not replace condoms, HIV testing or other prevention options.



It should be performed by trained healthcare professionals in an appropriate medical setting.



\### 4.7 Preventing Transmission Through Blood and Injecting Equipment



HIV prevention also involves reducing exposure to contaminated blood.



Important measures include:



\- Never share needles, syringes or other injecting equipment.

\- Use sterile medical equipment and approved injection services.

\- Ensure blood for transfusion is screened through appropriate safety systems.

\- Avoid unsterile instruments used for cutting, piercing or other procedures.

\- Healthcare workers should follow standard infection-prevention precautions.



People who inject drugs should be able to access appropriate harm-reduction and healthcare services without stigma or discrimination.



\### 4.8 Preventing Mother-to-Child Transmission



HIV can be transmitted from a mother living with HIV to her child during pregnancy, childbirth or breastfeeding.



Appropriate treatment and prevention services can greatly reduce this risk.



Important educational points:



\- HIV testing during pregnancy is important.

\- Pregnant women diagnosed with HIV should be linked promptly to appropriate treatment and care.

\- Antiretroviral treatment and recommended maternal and infant interventions help reduce transmission.

\- Feeding and follow-up decisions should follow current national guidance and individual professional assessment.



The chatbot must not give individualized pregnancy or infant-feeding instructions.



\## 5. Combining HIV Prevention Methods



No single prevention method is suitable for every person or situation.



People can discuss different prevention options with qualified healthcare professionals. Depending on individual circumstances, options may include:



\- Correct and consistent condom use.

\- Regular HIV testing.

\- PrEP where appropriate.

\- Prompt access to PEP after possible exposure.

\- HIV treatment and viral-load monitoring for people living with HIV.

\- Voluntary medical male circumcision.

\- Safe medical practices and sterile injecting equipment.

\- Maternal and infant HIV prevention services.



Combining suitable methods can provide additional protection.



\## 6. When Should Someone Seek Medical Care?



A person should seek medical assessment when:



\- They may have been exposed to HIV within the previous 72 hours.

\- They want to access HIV testing.

\- They are considering starting or stopping PrEP.

\- They have questions about HIV test results.

\- They are pregnant and need HIV testing or prevention services.

\- They have concerns about possible exposure through blood or injecting equipment.

\- They need support accessing HIV treatment or other prevention services.



Possible HIV exposure should not be judged by the presence or absence of symptoms. Testing and professional assessment are necessary.



\## 7. Common HIV Prevention Misconceptions



\*\*Misconception: HIV spreads through hugging or sharing food.\*\*



Correction: HIV is not transmitted through ordinary social contact, hugging or sharing food.



\*\*Misconception: A person can identify HIV infection by looking at someone.\*\*



Correction: HIV status cannot be determined by appearance. Testing is required.



\*\*Misconception: PEP can be started at any time after exposure.\*\*



Correction: PEP is time-sensitive. It should be started as soon as possible and no later than 72 hours after a possible exposure.



\*\*Misconception: PrEP and PEP are the same.\*\*



Correction: PrEP is used before possible exposure, while PEP is used after possible exposure.



\*\*Misconception: Condoms are the only HIV prevention option.\*\*



Correction: Several prevention methods are available, including PrEP, PEP, HIV testing, treatment and other prevention services.



\*\*Misconception: A person living with HIV who has an undetectable viral load can sexually transmit HIV.\*\*



Correction: A person who takes ART and achieves and maintains an undetectable viral load does not transmit HIV through sex (U=U).



\## 8. Safety and Ethical Considerations



The chatbot must:



\- Use respectful, inclusive and non-judgmental language.

\- Protect user privacy and avoid requesting unnecessary personal information.

\- Never assume someone's HIV status, sexual behaviour or identity.

\- Never diagnose HIV or interpret individual laboratory results.

\- Never prescribe PrEP, PEP or ART.

\- Never discourage HIV testing or professional medical assessment.

\- Direct possible exposures within 72 hours to urgent medical assessment.

\- Avoid inventing health facilities, contact details or service availability.

\- Use current, approved sources for clinical claims.

\- Clearly communicate uncertainty when evidence is insufficient.
