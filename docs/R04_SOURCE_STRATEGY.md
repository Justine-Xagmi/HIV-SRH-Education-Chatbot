## 1. Purpose

This document defines how authoritative sources will be identified, evaluated, recorded, approved and maintained for the HIV/SRH Education Chatbot.

The strategy prioritizes applicable Tanzanian Ministry of Health guidance, supported by relevant WHO technical guidance and carefully selected UNFPA resources.

A source being listed in this document does not mean that its publications, recommendations or extracted content have been approved for chatbot retrieval.

## 2. Source Selection Principles

Every candidate source must be assessed for:

1. Publisher authority and credibility.
2. Applicability to Tanzania.
3. Clinical accuracy and consistency with applicable national guidance.
4. Publication, revision and update dates.
5. Relevance to the chatbot's approved topics.
6. Language and translation suitability.
7. Permission to access, download, extract, store, translate and reuse the material.
8. Availability of reliable bibliographic metadata.
9. Traceability to the original publication.
10. Suitability for the intended general educational audience.

National guidance should normally take precedence for Tanzania-specific clinical and programme recommendations.

WHO guidance provides international technical context. Where newer WHO recommendations differ from national guidance, the discrepancy must be referred for qualified clinical review rather than resolved automatically.

## 5. Source Verification Record

Every selected publication must have a source record containing:

- `source_id`
- `title`
- `organization`
- `url`
- `language`
- `version`
- `publication_date`
- `last_verified_at`
- `jurisdiction`
- `permission_status`
- `permission_evidence`
- `review_status`
- `reviewer_id`
- `reviewed_at`
- `topics`
- `verification_notes`

The project should preserve the exact publication edition and original URL used during content preparation.

Where a publication does not provide a particular metadata field, the absence must be recorded rather than guessed.

## 6. Source Permission and Licensing

Source authority and content-use permission are separate assessments.

Before downloading, extracting, storing, translating or redistributing content, the project must assess the applicable terms.

The permission assessment must consider:
- Copyright ownership.
- Licence type and conditions.
- Attribution requirements.
- Restrictions on reproduction.
- Restrictions on derivative works and translation.
- Restrictions on commercial or public redistribution.
- Any permission required from the publisher.

A publicly accessible PDF or webpage is not automatically free to reproduce or redistribute.

If permission is unknown or insufficient, the material must not be marked as permitted for ingestion.

The project should record evidence supporting each permission decision.

## 7. Handling Conflicting Recommendations

When national and international recommendations differ:

1. Identify the exact publications and versions.
2. Record the relevant recommendation and publication date.
3. Establish whether the difference is substantive or contextual.
4. Check for newer national guidance or official updates.
5. Refer the issue to the designated qualified clinical reviewer.
6. Record the review decision and rationale.
7. Do not publish an unresolved clinical recommendation through chatbot retrieval.

The chatbot must not independently choose between conflicting clinical recommendations.

## 8. Source Status Lifecycle

Candidate sources should follow a controlled lifecycle:

- `pending_review`: identified but not fully assessed.
- `approved`: source metadata and permissions have passed the required review.
- `retired`: source is no longer eligible for new ingestion or retrieval.

Source approval does not automatically approve every document published by that organization.

Each document must undergo its own content and clinical review.

## 9. Source Maintenance

The project must establish a source-monitoring process that:

- Checks official publisher portals for updated publications.
- Records the date of each verification.
- Identifies revised, withdrawn or superseded publications.
- Flags knowledge documents affected by source updates.
- Prevents ineligible material from remaining retrievable.
- Preserves source and document history.
- Requires appropriate re-review before updated content is approved.

The exact verification frequency remains a governance decision and must consider source update practices and clinical risk.
